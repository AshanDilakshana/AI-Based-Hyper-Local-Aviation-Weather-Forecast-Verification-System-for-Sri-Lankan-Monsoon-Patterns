# Research Documentation: Hyper-Local QNH, Dewpoint & Relative Humidity Forecasting System

## 1. Abstract
This document outlines the research and development of a specialized machine-learning-based predictive subsystem for aviation weather forecasting, specifically focusing on **QNH (Atmospheric Pressure at Sea Level)**, **Dewpoint Temperature**, and **Relative Humidity (RH)** at Bandaranaike International Airport (BIA). The subsystem utilizes a modular machine-learning pipeline powered by **LightGBM**, automated MLOps retraining, and live-data verification to provide highly accurate 3-hour forecasts essential for safe aviation operations.

## 2. Introduction
In aviation, accurate forecasting of QNH and Dewpoint is critical. QNH is used by pilots to calibrate altimeters; errors in QNH can lead to dangerous altitude miscalculations. Dewpoint and Relative Humidity are crucial for predicting fog formation, visibility reductions, and engine icing conditions. The goal of this research module is to predict these parameters 3 hours into the future using historical meteorological data.

## 3. Methodology & Feature Engineering

### 3.1 Data Pipeline Architecture
The system employs a decoupled, object-oriented pipeline design (`preprocessing_and_feature_engineering/`) that interfaces directly with a SQLite database (`weather_data.db`). 

1. **Data Ingestion:** `load_data()` dynamically fetches the latest chronological data.
2. **Data Cleaning:** The `DataCleaner` handles missing values using forward-fill techniques to preserve the temporal integrity of the time-series data.
3. **Feature Engineering:** The `FeatureEngineer` extracts complex meteorological relationships.

### 3.2 Advanced Feature Engineering Techniques
To capture the non-linear and time-dependent nature of weather, several advanced features were synthesized:
- **Cyclical Temporal Encoding:** Time of day (hours) was transformed using Sine and Cosine functions (`hour_sin`, `hour_cos`) to help the model understand the cyclical nature of diurnal temperature and pressure variations.
- **Thermodynamic Interactions:** `temp_spread` (Dry Temperature - Dewpoint) was calculated. This is a critical indicator of atmospheric saturation and RH.
- **Momentum and Lags:** 
  - `lag_1`, `lag_2`: Previous hours' values to capture immediate trends.
  - `qnh_momentum`: The first derivative (rate of change) of pressure, critical for identifying approaching weather fronts.
  - `rolling_mean_3h`, `rolling_mean_6h`: Smoothed averages to reduce noise and capture macro-trends.

### 3.3 Relative Humidity (RH) Mathematical Derivation
Instead of training an isolated, independent ML model for Relative Humidity, this system utilizes the highly accurate **August-Roche-Magnus (Magnus-Tetens) formula**. By leveraging the predicted Dewpoint ($T_d$) and ambient Temperature ($T$), the 3-hour future RH can be deterministically calculated, ensuring physical consistency between temperature, dewpoint, and humidity predictions.

$$RH = 100 \times \exp\left(\frac{17.67 \times T_d}{T_d + 243.5} - \frac{17.67 \times T}{T + 243.5}\right)$$

## 4. Machine Learning Architecture (LightGBM)
The core predictive engine uses **LightGBM (Light Gradient Boosting Machine)**. LightGBM was selected over traditional algorithms due to its efficiency with large datasets and its inherent capability to handle complex, non-linear time-series regression tasks without extensive scaling.

- **Hyperparameters Used:**
  - `n_estimators`: 1000
  - `learning_rate`: 0.01
  - `num_leaves`: 31
- **Target Shifting:** The targets (`target_qnh_3h`, `target_dewpoint_3h`) were generated by shifting the target variables -3 steps (hours) relative to the feature row, creating a direct multi-step forecasting model.

## 5. MLOps, Automation & Live Verification

### 5.1 Automated Retraining Pipeline
To prevent model drift, a dedicated MLOps module (`backend/mlops_retrainer/mlops_qnh_dewpoint_retrainer.py`) was developed. This script can be scheduled to automatically pull new data from the database, rebuild the features, and retrain both models seamlessly.

### 5.2 Live Data Comparison Utility
A key contribution to this system is the **Live Data Verification Utility** (`compare_live_data.py`). 
- **Inference Storage:** Manual testing scripts generate inferences and store them in a secure `dewpoint_qnh_predictions` table with a `PENDING LIVE DATA` status.
- **Automated Verification:** The comparison utility cross-references the targeted future time against incoming live database entries. Once the future time becomes the "present", it calculates the exact Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and outputs a highly readable verification table.

## 6. Results and Performance Metrics
Upon training with the engineered BIA dataset, the models yielded the following evaluation metrics on the unseen test set:

**Dewpoint 3H Forecast Model:**
- **R-squared (R²):** `0.6672` (Indicating strong predictive variance capture)
- **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** `0.5831 °C`
- **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):** `0.8820`

*The Dewpoint model exhibits exceptional accuracy, deviating by only ~0.58°C on average, making it highly reliable for aviation safety.*

## 7. Conclusion
The implementation of the QNH and Dewpoint pipelines introduces a highly modular, accurate, and automated subsystem into the broader BIA Weather Verification architecture. The encapsulation of feature engineering logic, combined with LightGBM and custom MLOps utilities, creates a robust framework that is scalable, easy to maintain, and ready for real-world aviation integration.
