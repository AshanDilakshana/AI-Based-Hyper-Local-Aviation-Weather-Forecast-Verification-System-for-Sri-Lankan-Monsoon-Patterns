## 1. Introduction
In aviation, thermodynamic parameters such as temperature and atmospheric pressure dictate aircraft performance and safety. Density Altitude—the altitude relative to the standard atmosphere at which the air density would be equal to the observed air density—directly impacts lift generation and engine thrust. Furthermore, the thermodynamic relationship between temperature drops and pressure stability serves as the primary catalyst for severe aviation hazards, notably radiation fog.

Current meteorological systems at Bandaranaike International Airport (VCBI) rely on broad-scale numerical weather prediction (NWP) models which often fail to capture hyper-local variations. This research addresses this gap by developing an AI-driven, hyper-local verification layer that autonomously predicts T+3 hour thermodynamic states using real-time METAR data.

## 2. Methodology
The system architecture encompasses data ingestion, hybrid machine learning prediction, derived physics computation, and an automated continuous training (MLOps) pipeline.

### 2.1 Dataset and Feature Engineering
A historical dataset comprising over 120,000 hourly METAR records for the VCBI region was utilized. The data specifically focuses on the Northeast Monsoon period. Advanced feature engineering was applied to extract temporal dynamics:
* **Lag Features:** Previous hours' temperature and pressure states to capture inertia.
* **Rolling Averages:** To smooth micro-fluctuations in atmospheric pressure.
* **Psychrometric Variables:** Humidity, dew point, and wind vectors were included as independent variables impacting thermodynamic shifts.

### 2.2 Hybrid Machine Learning Architecture
To balance non-linear pattern recognition with time-series forecasting, a hybrid model was developed:
* **Base Model (Random Forest):** A Random Forest Regressor was employed to map the complex, non-linear relationships between variables (e.g., how wind direction interacts with humidity).
* **Temporal Model (LSTM):** A Long Short-Term Memory network processes the sequential nature of the data, correcting temporal errors generated by the base model.
* **Fusion Strategy:** The outputs of both models are weighted and combined, producing a highly stable prediction optimized for a 3-hour lead time.

### 2.3 Derived Aviation Physics Engine
Raw predictions are programmatically translated into actionable aviation metrics. The system calculates:
1. **Pressure Altitude:** Computed using the standard lapse rate.
2. **ISA Temperature Deviation:** Evaluating the predicted temperature against the International Standard Atmosphere.
3. **Density Altitude:** Used to classify the airport's operational state into `NORMAL`, `ELEVATED`, or `CRITICAL` risk bands.

### 2.4 Thermodynamic Hazard Precursor Verification (Radiation Fog)
A novel contribution of this research is the proactive detection of Radiation Fog *precursors* rather than predicting the fog itself. Statistical analysis of the VCBI dataset revealed that severe fog correlates strongly with specific thermodynamic conditions.
The system triggers an independent **HIGH RISK (>85% Probability)** alert under a coupled anomaly:
1. **Rapid Radiative Cooling:** A temperature drop of ≥ 1.0°C over 3 hours (Rate of Change ≤ -0.33°C/hr).
2. **High & Stable Pressure:** QNH in the 75th percentile for VCBI (≥ 1012.0 hPa) with minimal hourly fluctuation (≤ 0.5 hPa).

### 2.5 MLOps and Continuous Training Pipeline
To prevent concept drift due to shifting global climate patterns, a Continuous Training (CT) pipeline was deployed.
* **Automated Background Scheduler:** Triggers monthly retraining cycles using newly acquired METAR data.
* **Hot-Reloading:** Integrates the newly generated model weights into the FastAPI backend memory dynamically, ensuring zero downtime for the verification service.
